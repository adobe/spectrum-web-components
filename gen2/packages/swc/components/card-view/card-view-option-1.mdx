## Anatomy

`swc-card-view-option-1` implements the historical RSP recommendation: one semantic row per card, containing a focusable `rowheader`. Each card contains a photo, checkbox, primary action, and Share action. The collection reports one semantic column regardless of visual layout.

## Options

### Grid

Cards align in uniform rows. Left/Right visits enabled cards in collection order; Up/Down uses spatial overlap.

<Canvas of={Stories.Grid} />

### Waterfall

Cards are placed into the shortest column using their measured heights. Left/Right remains sequential even when the next card is not a visual neighbor.

<Canvas of={Stories.Waterfall} />

## Behaviors

Space toggles selection; Enter opens the focused card. Home/End reaches the first/last enabled card. Shift plus an arrow selects a collection-order range. Tab visits the active card's checkbox and actions, then exits the collection. Child actions do not also select the card.

## Accessibility

### Features

Row count and row index describe collection positions, not visual rows. Selected states and a selected-count status communicate selection. Disabled cards are skipped. Resize measurement changes visual positions without changing semantic structure or replacing the focused item.

<Canvas of={Stories.Accessibility} />

### Pros

- Provides a predictable sequential route to every enabled card in both layouts.
- Retains grid selection and independently interactive children.
- Closely follows the original RSP keyboard recommendation rather than the waterfall baseline's divergence.

### Cons

- In observed screen reader output, every card is read as "1 of 1" rather than its position in the full collection.
- Down still uses geometry and can stop before every card has been visited.
- The single-column announcement does not convey the visual vertical-navigation model.
- Sequential horizontal movement in waterfall may look surprising, and position announcements remain screen reader dependent.

### Best practices

This is a testable hypothesis, not an accessibility sign-off. Evaluate whether users discover Left/Right as the complete traversal route without changing screen reader settings. Test Tab and Shift+Tab entry/exit, controls, disabled items, zoom, and position announcements across the documented matrix.
