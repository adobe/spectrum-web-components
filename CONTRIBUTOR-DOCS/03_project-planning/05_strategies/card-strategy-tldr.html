The single-column grid · Six variations

Looks like many.
Announces as one.

A visual wall of cards, but one semantic column. Each card gets a row. The screen reader's map and the arrow keys' route may tell different fortunes.

Watch the mismatch: “one column” does not explain why moving down misses cards in other visual columns.

  • 1A The RSP baseline. Left/Right is sequential in grid, spatial in waterfall. Up/Down is spatial.
  • 1B Follow the geometry. All arrows are spatial. No wrapping.
  • 1C Stay in the circle. Spatial arrows wrap within the same aligned visual row or column.
  • 1D The historical RSP contract. Sequential Left/Right, spatial Up/Down. Uses rowheaders.
  • 1E The eternal return. Each arrow cycles through the entire enabled collection.
  • 1F Next column, please. Sequential Left/Right; Up/Down follows columns, continuing into the next one.

Three bad omens

The visuals say grid.
The voices say what?

Pretty cards are not the problem.
A confusing map is.

  1. The vanishing cards

    Down can stop at a visual column's bottom before every card is visited. Has the collection ended, or is there another route?

  2. The unreliable prophecy

    Reported speech includes “1 of 1” and inconsistent position feedback. ARIA metadata alone cannot promise “7 of 100.”

  3. The shifting floor

    Waterfall changes the geometry. Virtualization adds loading, reading-order, and focus risks. Test those separately.

Know your navigation spells

Not all circles lead everywhere.

“Wrapping” can mean two very different things. One circles a visual band. The other visits the whole collection.

The compass

Spatial arrows choose a nearby aligned card. At the edge, stop. Repeating one key will not visit everything.

1B + 2B · Spatial, no wrap

The small circle

At the edge, return to the other end of the same aligned row or column. You can loop without seeing the rest.

1C + 2C · Local wrapping

The thread

Left/Right follows collection order; Up/Down follows geometry. The sequential route stops at collection boundaries.

1D + 2A · One sequential pair

The eternal return

Every arrow follows collection order and cycles. Any one repeated arrow visits every enabled item from any start.

1E + 2D · Full cycling

Spatial alignment uses overlap on the perpendicular axis. In waterfall, a “row” is a visual band, not necessarily a neat horizontal line. Sequential horizontal directions mirror in RTL.

Your future is not yet selected

The winning card?
Ask the people.

A passing axe scan is not a blessing from the accessibility universe. Neither is “Google does it.” The winner must work for keyboard and screen reader users.

Start with a repaired layout grid versus a native list. No option is approved yet.

  1. First, a fair reading. Use a fully loaded, uniform layout with identical content and actions.
  2. Make the task real. Find card seven, visit every card, select two, and use a bulk action.
  3. Keep the spells separate. Selecting a card, opening it, and using its child controls must not trigger one another.
  4. Then shake the table. Test waterfall, resizing, removal, and focus visibility. Add virtualization last.

Read the leaves, then the receipts

Guidance, not gospel.

APG permits layout grids and optional wrapping. The original RSP spec informs 1D; current RSP and Piyush's PR inform 1A. Reported Google Drive semantics inspire the single-row experiments.

Higley and Roselli caution against choosing a grid just because it looks like one or saves tab stops. A feed suits reading; a toolbar suits commands. The role should match the task.

The non-negotiables

Good fortune is usable.

  • Every enabled card and child control is reachable.
  • Focus stays visible, stable, and clear of action bars.
  • Position, total, and selected state are tested in real speech.
  • Keyboard users can enter, leave, and reverse their route.

Test NVDA, JAWS, VoiceOver, Narrator, mobile screen readers, and keyboard-only use. Reports are not independently verified findings. Automated tests do not establish usability or WCAG conformance.
